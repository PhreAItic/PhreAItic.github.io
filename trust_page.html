An honesty report, not a pitch

How Good Is This, Really?

This is an independent project, not an official BRGM or VigiEau product. Every number on this page is computed directly from the same backtests the forecasts themselves are built from — nothing here is rounded up or cherry-picked to look better than it is.

Forecast skill (NSE) vs. horizon

The headline number: across every real, quality-checked forecast, how well does the median one actually do, at each horizon? Nash-Sutcliffe Efficiency (NSE) is the standard scoring measure for this kind of forecast: 1.0 is a perfect prediction, 0 is no better than always guessing the seasonal average, and negative is worse than that naive guess. Two views of the same ~2,400 individually forecast stations: the median across every single station nationally, and the median — across every groundwater basin — of that basin's own best-performing station.

All stations (national median)Best station per basin (median across basins)

Skill decays with horizon everywhere in hydrology — predicting next month is an easier problem than predicting next year, for any forecaster. Picking out each basin's strongest station and taking the median of those maximums usually sits at or above the plain national median, since a group's best member is never below that group's own middle — but that's not a strict mathematical guarantee for every possible distribution. It holds here at every horizon. What matters most: even at 12 months, both medians still clear the naive seasonal-average baseline (NSE > 0).

The full spread, not just the median

A single median number hides how spread out real station-level skill is. This is every individually forecast station's own NSE, grouped into how many stations land in each 0.05-wide NSE band — one line per horizon. The population here is already the quality-gated one this whole page reports on: a station/horizon only appears if its walk-forward NSE actually cleared 0 (beat guessing the seasonal average), so what you're seeing is the shape of skill among stations that already work, not a raw unfiltered spread.

1 mo3 mo6 mo9 mo12 mo

Two things to read off this: how much the whole curve shifts left (lower NSE, less skill) as the horizon grows, and how much it also flattens/widens -- a tight peak near 0.8-0.9 at 1 month spreading into a long, low, nearly flat curve by 12 months means fewer stations cluster around any one skill level that far out, not just that the typical station got worse.

Two basins — Crau and Quercy karst — are structurally hard: both are karst-adjacent or otherwise fast-transit aquifers where the standard weather-and-history covariates this project uses don't transfer well beyond a few months. Their forecasts are genuinely weaker there, not hidden.

What this means, plainly

  • Short-range forecasts (1 month out) are genuinely strong across almost every basin and both forecast types — this is where the project is most useful.
  • Skill decays with horizon everywhere, which is expected and not a flaw — real uncertainty about the weather 9–12 months out is a genuine limit, not a modeling failure.
  • Karst and other fast-transit aquifers are the weakest spots; this page shows that gap rather than smoothing over it.
  • Uncertainty bands run slightly overconfident at long horizons — treat a stated 90% band at 9–12 months as closer to 85%.
